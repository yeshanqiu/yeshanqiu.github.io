Naturalistic roadside green space

Earlier work on ecologically informed green space design, published in Chinese. It relates planting configurations to green-space ratio and green-view ratio, raising ecological quality while keeping public aesthetic appeal.

Field site
Roadside green space, Shanghai

Two layers, or three

The usual roadside section is a street tree over clipped shrub: tidy, cheap to cut, and almost empty as habitat. A compound community keeps the same width of ground and adds the layer that has been left out, so light, shelter, and food occur at several heights instead of one.

Left, the conventional section. Right, the compound tree group: canopy, shrub, and groundcover in one community, native species over eighty per cent.

Six configuration modes

Each mode sets out what the community is made of, how it is layered, and what vertical structure results, so that a designer can specify one without guessing at proportions. The compound tree group in the last row is the fullest case: tree, shrub, and groundcover in one community.

Ecological quality without losing legibility

Roadside planting is usually simplified to a few species in flat layers, which is easy to maintain and poor as habitat. The study sets out compound communities that layer tree, shrub, and groundcover, so structural diversity rises while the planting still reads as cared for to the people who pass it every day.
